# BuildFlow — Contractor Discovery Outreach and Interview Notes

**Status:** Draft for internal review. Not sent to any organisation.  
**Use:** Individual research invitations only; not a product launch announcement.

## 1. Email invitation

**Subject:** Request for a short construction project-management research conversation

Hello [Name],

I’m conducting research into how construction businesses coordinate project work across the office, procurement and site teams.

I would appreciate 20–30 minutes to understand how your team currently handles project tasks, BOQs, purchase requests and approvals, site progress updates and client reporting—particularly where information gets delayed, duplicated or difficult to track.

This is a research conversation rather than a sales presentation. I’m using the feedback to validate the requirements for a construction operations platform that is being developed. There is no commitment to use or purchase anything.

Would you be open to a short conversation at a time convenient to you?

Kind regards,  
[Sender name]  
[Business/contact details]

Hello [Name], just following up on my earlier request for a short research conversation about construction project coordination. If this is relevant to your role, I’d appreciate 20–30 minutes at a convenient time. If not, no problem at all. Thank you.

## 4. Interview opening and consent

- Explain the purpose: understand current workflows and operational pain points.
- Explain how notes will be used and who will have access.
- Ask permission before recording; do not record by default.
- Do not request confidential project documents, client information, employee records or sensitive financial data.
- Allow the participant to skip any question.
- Do not promise product functionality, launch dates, pricing or pilot availability.

## 6. Interview scoring — internal only

Score each 1–5, and record evidence rather than relying on intuition.

A good pilot candidate is not simply the most enthusiastic respondent. Prefer a clear operational problem, a suitable project, an accountable sponsor, manageable data sensitivity and workflows that the accepted MVP can support.

## 7. After each interview

1. Separate direct quotes from interpretation.
2. Record the top three pains and current workaround.
3. Identify which MVP workflow step addresses each pain.
4. Record gaps honestly; do not promise a feature because it was requested.
5. Update the product prioritisation backlog only after comparing evidence across interviews.
6. Ask permission before adding the contact to ongoing communications.
7. Do not identify the participant publicly without explicit written permission.
